Why

Songs finished months apart rarely match.

Songs recorded, mixed and mastered at different times tend to end up at different loudness. Put them in order and one song jumps out while the next sounds small. Matching them by ear means playing back and forth and nudging faders, then doing it all again after a remaster.

Album Leveler measures each song the way loudness meters do (integrated LUFS, following the ITU-R BS.1770 method). Then it sets every track to a shared target in one step. It only changes level. Your EQ, dynamics and master stay as they are.

Built for sequencing an album, not one song.

How it works

Five steps, about one album's listening time.

  1. Insert it on every song

    Add Album Leveler to each song's stereo track, last in the chain. Each instance takes its own track slot automatically.

  2. Pick a target

    Set the same Target loudness and Maximum correction on every instance. The default is -11 LUFS with a 6 dB limit.

  3. Reset, then play

    Press Reset all analysis, then play each song from start to finish. The status shows Analysing… while it measures.

  4. Calculate

    Press Calculate album levels on any instance. Every track's gain is set at once, and the Album tab shows the result.

  5. Listen and refine

    Play through the album in order. Loudness gets you close; your ears make the final call.

FAQ

Questions

Does it change the sound of my songs?

No. It only applies a clean gain change per track. There's no EQ, compression or limiting.

Is it a limiter? Can it clip?

It isn't a limiter. If a song gets a positive gain that could push its sample peak above 0 dBFS, the plug-in warns you ("May clip"). Peaks are sample peaks, not true peaks, so check your final master with a true-peak meter.

Do I have to play every song all the way through?

For the most accurate result, yes. Integrated loudness describes a whole song. Playing part of a song still gives a reading, but it only represents the part you played.

What happens when I play a song again after calculating?

Loudness is measured before the plug-in's own gain, so the correction isn't measured twice. If new analysis arrives after a calculation, the plug-in tells you, and you can press Calculate again.

Is it a certified loudness meter?

No. It follows the ITU-R BS.1770 method (K-weighting, gating, channel summing) and measures standard test signals within 0.02 LU, but it hasn't been through formal EBU conformance testing. It's built for matching songs to each other, not for delivery compliance.

Why does a mono track read 3 dB quieter?

That's how the loudness standard works: a mono track measures 3 dB lower than the same audio on both channels of a stereo track. Use stereo tracks so every song is measured the same way.

How do updates work?

Once a day the plug-in checks a small version file on this website. Nothing about you or your project is sent. If a newer version is out, a button appears at the top of the plug-in that opens this page. Nothing is installed automatically. You can switch the check off on the Help tab.

Are my settings saved?

Yes. Each instance stores its slot, settings, name, analysis and gain in the Logic project.
